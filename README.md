# Haath Se (हाथ से)

A web app that teaches Indian Sign Language (ISL) with your webcam. Learners first read the rights of Deaf people in India, then learn ISL numbers and the alphabet. The camera checks each sign in real time, and every sign learned unlocks a fact about Deaf people and ISL in India.

Built at CodeDay Bengaluru, Fall 2026.

## The problem

India has over 50 lakh people with a hearing disability (Census 2011), and the law recognises their right to education and information in sign language. But very few hearing people know ISL, and most learning resources are passive videos with no feedback.

## What it does

- **Your rights (shown first):** plain-language summaries of the RPwD Act 2016 (Sections 3, 16, 17, 32, 34, 42), NEP 2020 para 4.22 and the UN CRPD, with links to the official text.
- **Learn:** three units following ISLRTC: numbers 1 to 9, letters A to Z, and 30 everyday expressions (Hello, Namaste, Good morning, Thank you, Please, Sorry, Yes, No, Good, Bad, Help, Stop, Wait, Come, Go, Again, Understand, Name, Water, Eat, Drink, Toilet, Doctor, Home, School, Friend, Family, Mother, Father, Love). Each country has its own sign language, so these are the ISL signs; for signs with movement, the camera checks the main hand shape. Hold the sign for one second and the ring fills. Signs not yet set up for camera checking can still be learned from ISLRTC with an "I've got it" button.
- **Awareness facts:** every sign you learn unlocks one of 13 sourced facts and etiquette tips.
- **Practice:** 10 signs from memory, with stars for speed.
- **Sign Rush:** pop rising bubbles by signing, with a shared leaderboard.
- **Organiser setup (`/setup`, PIN protected):** record each sign once so the camera can check it.

## Run it

You need Python 3.10+.

```bash
cd backend
pip install -r requirements.txt
uvicorn server:app --reload --port 8000
```

Open http://localhost:8000. The terminal prints the **setup PIN**; or choose one with `SETUP_PIN=123456 uvicorn server:app --port 8000`.

The camera only works on **https** or **localhost**. To let other phones and laptops join, deploy it (for example on Render or Railway) or make an https tunnel with `npx localtunnel --port 8000` or `cloudflared tunnel --url http://localhost:8000`.

### Storage

- No setup: data is saved to `backend/data.json`.
- MongoDB: set `MONGO_URL` (for example a free MongoDB Atlas cluster) and optionally `DB_NAME`.

## Built-in signs (no recording needed)

Numbers 1-9 and letters A-Z work out of the box. Each has a picture, an animated hand outline, a guide outline drawn on the learner's camera, and about 50 hand-tracking examples for checking. They were generated by `tools/build_builtin_signs.py` from the MIT-licensed ISL chart by Karthikeyu Busa and Shravani Katte (github.com/Karthikeyu/Indian-sign-language-recognition): the script crops each sign, runs MediaPipe Hand Landmarker on ~54 variations (scale, padding, rotation, mirrored), and writes `frontend/js/builtin-signs.js`.

## Before the demo: record the expressions

1. Open `/setup`, enter the PIN and your name.
2. Start the camera. Look each sign up in ISLRTC's **Sign Learn** app (Android/iOS) or the [ISLRTC dictionary](https://islrtc.nic.in/).
3. Press **1. Demo clip** and do the full sign for 3 seconds, including any movement. Learners see this clip, mirrored, in the "Copy this" card.
4. Press **2. Hand shape** and hold the main shape still for 3 seconds. Do this 2 or 3 times, ideally with different people. A sign needs 10+ samples for camera checking; 60+ shows as Ready.
5. Demo clips are saved as files in `backend/media/`.
6. **Export sign pack** to keep a backup of hand shapes (demo clips stay in `backend/media/`). Packs exported from the single-file Haath Se can be imported too.

## How recognition works

1. **MediaPipe Hand Landmarker** finds 21 points on each hand, up to two hands, in the browser.
2. Each hand's points are made relative to the wrist and scaled by hand size, so distance from the camera doesn't matter. Two-hand signs also include where the hands are relative to each other: 129 numbers per frame.
3. **K-nearest neighbours** (k = 7) compares those numbers with the recorded samples. If the closest match is farther than the tolerance, it reports no sign.
4. A sign counts only if it wins 4 of the last 7 frames and is held (1 s in Learn, 0.6 s in Practice, 0.35 s in Sign Rush).

No video or camera frames are sent to the server. Only hand-point numbers, one small reference photo per sign, and leaderboard scores are stored.

## API

| Method | Path | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| GET | `/api/signs` | Recording status and reference photo for every sign |
| GET | `/api/samples` | All recorded samples |
| POST | `/api/samples` | Add samples (PIN) |
| DELETE | `/api/signs/{id}` | Clear a sign's samples and photo (PIN) |
| PUT | `/api/signs/{id}/ref` | Set a reference photo (PIN) |
| POST | `/api/setup/check` | Check the PIN |
| GET / POST | `/api/leaderboard`, `/api/scores` | Sign Rush scores |

PIN-protected routes take the PIN in the `X-Setup-Pin` header.

### Built-in expressions

The 30 everyday expressions (Hello, Welcome, Good morning, Good night, Thank you, Please, Sorry, Yes, No, Good, Bad, Help, Stop, Wait, Come, Go, Again, Understand, Name, Eat, Drink, Hungry, Thirsty, Home, School, Friend, Mother, Father, Happy, Bye) are built in as **animated figures** with a hand-shape close-up, plus camera checking. `tools/build_expressions.py` measured the hand movement frame by frame (MediaPipe Hand Landmarker) from community 3D avatar ISL clips in [Rohitkumar-3103/Indian_Sign_Language_Projects](https://github.com/Rohitkumar-3103/Indian_Sign_Language_Projects). That repository has no licence and doesn't name who made the clips, so **no video is copied**: only hand-point numbers are stored, in `frontend/js/builtin-expressions.js`. These avatar signs are not verified by ISLRTC, so confirm them before teaching others.

### How-to-sign steps

Every built-in sign has numbered "How to sign it" steps, generated by `tools/build_instructions.py` from the same hand data: which hand(s), hand shape (which fingers are straight), where the hand goes, and how it moves. They describe what the picture or animation shows, so they always match the demo, and the app links to ISLRTC for the official form.

## Known limits

- Static signs only. Signs that involve movement aren't recognised yet.
- Recognition is only as good as the recordings. Record with several people's hands and good lighting.
- Legal summaries are for awareness, not legal advice.
